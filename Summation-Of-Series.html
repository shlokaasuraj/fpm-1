The Clever Lego Staircase Trick

Imagine building a staircase of toy blocks: step 1 has 1 block, step 2 has 2 blocks, up to step $n$.

How do you count all the blocks in one second without counting one-by-one?

The Magic Twin Mirror: Make an identical duplicate staircase, flip it upside down, and snap them together! They form a perfect rectangle of height $n$ and width $(n+1)$!

$\text{Two Staircases } = n \times (n+1) \implies \text{One Staircase } = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$

  • Factorising vs Expanding: Factorise terms like $n(n+1)$ directly instead of expanding into cubics.

The Falling Dominoes Effect

Imagine a line of 100 soldiers holding hands: $(+A - B) + (+B - C) + (+C - D) + \dots$

Each soldier's left hand cancels out the previous soldier's right hand! The entire giant middle collapses into zero!

Only the very first soldier's left hand and the very last soldier's right hand survive!

The Shrinking Crumb Trail

Can you add numbers together forever and never reach infinity?

YES! Look at the leftover tail in our formula:

$S_n = 1 - \frac{1}{n+1}$

When $n = 1,000,000$, $\frac{1}{1,000,001}$ is smaller than a speck of dust! As $n \to \infty$, the fraction evaporates to zero, leaving exactly the sum $S_\infty = 1$!

Cambridge Specification Convergence:

Alternating Squares: $1^2 - 2^2 + 3^2 - 4^2 + \dots - (2n)^2$

Group into differences of two squares: $(1-2)(1+2) + (3-4)(3+4) + \dots$ Each pair has factor $-1$, so the total is $- (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + \dots + 2n) = - \frac{2n(2n+1)}{2} = -n(2n+1)$!

June 2009 Past Paper 9231 P1 Q2

Double Denominator Telescoping: $S_\infty = \frac{1}{15}$

$\frac{4n+9}{(n+2)(n+3)(2n+3)(2n+5)} = \frac{1}{(n+2)(2n+3)} - \frac{1}{(n+3)(2n+5)}$. All middle terms cancel, leaving $S_N = \frac{1}{15} - \frac{1}{(N+3)(2N+5)}$. As $N \to \infty$, $S_\infty = \frac{1}{15}$!

June 2013 Past Paper 9231 P11 Q5

Inequality Tail Bound: $\sum_{r=N+1}^{2N} \dots < \frac{1}{8N}$

$\sum_{r=1}^N \frac{1}{(2r+1)(2r+3)} = \frac{1}{6} - \frac{1}{2(2N+3)}$. The sum from $N+1$ to $2N$ simplifies to $\frac{1}{2(2N+3)} - \frac{1}{2(4N+3)} = \frac{N}{(2N+3)(4N+3)} < \frac{N}{8N^2} = \frac{1}{8N}$!
