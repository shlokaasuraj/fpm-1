The Magical Train Track (Invariant Lines)

Most points get thrown completely sideways to new random directions when multiplied by a matrix.

An invariant line is like a set of train tracks passing through $(0,0)$. When the matrix transforms any point on this track, the point slides forward or backward, but never leaves the track!

$M \begin{pmatrix} t \\ mt \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} T \\ mT \end{pmatrix} \iff \text{Points on } y=mx \text{ stay on } y=mx$

The 3D Skewer Trick

When rotating in 3D around an axis (say the $z$-axis), imagine pushing a metal skewer right through the $z$-axis!

Anything on that skewer cannot move ($z' = z$). The top-left $2\times2$ block simply does regular 2D rotation on $x$ and $y$!

$R_z(\theta) = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta & 0 \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$

The Sock and Shoe Rule ($(AB)^{-1} = B^{-1}A^{-1}$)

In the morning, you put on your sock first (A), then your shoe second (B)!

At night, how do you undo it? You can't take your sock off through your shoe! You must take your shoe off first ($B^{-1}$), then your sock second ($A^{-1}$)!

$(AB)^{-1} = B^{-1} A^{-1} \quad (\text{Order Reverses!})$
Cambridge Singularity Checker:
Singular Condition: $\det(A) = 0$ If $\det(A) = 0$, dividing by zero in $A^{-1} = \frac{1}{\det(A)} \text{adj}(A)$ is impossible! The matrix is singular (non-invertible).
💡 Past Paper Shortcut: To find $(AB)^{-1}$, compute $AB$ first and invert that single matrix rather than finding two separate inverses!

Worked Ex 4.13 3D Plane Reflection

Reflection in Plane $x+y=0$

Looking down the $z$-axis, the plane is the line $y=-x$. Thus $x' = -y, y' = -x$, while $z' = z$ is unchanged! The reflection matrix is $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

Area Preservation Determinant = 1

Preserving Triangle Area Under $T$

For $T = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 3 \\ 1 & 2 \end{pmatrix}$, $\det(T) = 2(2) - 3(1) = 1$. Since $|\det(T)| = 1$, the area of transformed triangle (5,3), (13,7), (17,10) is strictly identical to original triangle (1,1), (5,1), (4,3)!
