The Magic Dominoes & The Infinite Ladder

Imagine an endless line of toy dominoes marching all the way to the moon! How can you be 100% sure that EVERY SINGLE DOMINO will fall over?

You only need two magical rules:

  • Rule 1 (The First Push): You must be able to knock down the very first domino ($P_1$).
  • Rule 2 (The Spacing Link): Whenever any domino $k$ falls, it is close enough to tap domino $(k+1)$ and knock it down too!
$P_1 \text{ is true} \quad \text{and} \quad P_k \Rightarrow P_{k+1} \implies \text{Every domino falls forever!}$

The Inductive Lego Tower Trick

Imagine you have already proved that a tower of $k$ Lego floors matches your secret formula $S_k$.

Now someone hands you the very next floor $T_{k+1}$! You don't start counting from floor 1 again! You simply take your known box $S_k$, snap the new block on top, and factor out common terms:

$S_{k+1} = \underbrace{S_k}_{\text{Assumed formula}} + \underbrace{T_{k+1}}_{\text{Next term}} \equiv S(k+1)$

Integer $n$: n = 2

The Clock Wheel That Always Hits Twelve

Imagine a round clock wheel divided into $d$ equal slices.

When $n=0$, our pointer lands right on the top mark at 12 o'clock (remainder 0)!

How do we know it never slips away as $n$ grows? Because the difference $f(k+1) - f(k)$ is always a whole number of complete spins ($d \times M$)!

$f(k+1) = f(k) + \text{Multiple of } d \implies \text{Remainder stays 0!}$

Double-Flipping Waves & Matrix Steps

Cambridge Proof Breakdown:
June 2014 P11 Q3 Divisibility by 24

$11^{2n} + 25^n + 22$ is Divisible by 24

Base case $n=0$: $1+1+22 = 24$. Difference $f(k+1) - f(k) = 11^{2k}(120) + 25^k(24) = 24[5(121^k) + 25^k]$, which is strictly divisible by 24!

Nov 2014 P11 Q3 Factorial Conjecture

Conjecture & Proof: $S_n = \sum r \cdot r!$

$2!-S_1 = 1, 3!-S_2 = 1, 4!-S_3 = 1 \implies$ conjecture $S_n = (n+1)! - 1$. Inductive step: $S_k + (k+1)(k+1)! = (k+1)![1 + k + 1] - 1 = (k+2)! - 1$!

June 2015 P11 Q3 Monotonic Decrease

$a_{n+1} = \frac{4a_n}{5} + \frac{5}{a_n} > 5$ & $a_n > a_{n+1}$

$a_{n+1} - 5 = \frac{(4a_n - 5)(a_n - 5)}{5a_n} > 0$. Also $a_n - a_{n+1} = \frac{a_n^2 - 25}{5a_n} > 0 \implies a_n$ strictly decreases toward floor 5!
