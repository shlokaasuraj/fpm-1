ELI6: The Cookie Monster's Secret Recipe

Imagine baking a magic polynomial cake using secret number ingredients called Roots ($\alpha, \beta, \gamma$). A root is where the roller coaster dips right into the water at height zero ($y=0$)!

Instead of writing the whole messy polynomial, you can know everything about it just by looking at two master tricks:

  • The Big Hug ($\Sigma \alpha$): Add all roots together! Notice the minus sign: the coefficient in front of $x^{n-1}$ is always the negative of this sum ($-b/a$).
  • The Great Banquet ($\Pi \alpha$): Multiply all roots together! For a cubic it flips sign to $-d/a$, and for a quartic it stays positive $+e/a$.
The Alternating Sign Traffic Light:
Negative (-) $\Sigma \alpha = -b/a$
Positive (+) $\Sigma \alpha\beta = +c/a$
Negative (-) $\alpha\beta\gamma = -d/a$

⚠️ Common Cambridge Examination Traps:
  • Missing Power Trap: If a cubic lacks an $x^2$ term (e.g. $x^3 - 7x + 6 = 0$), then $b=0 \implies \sum \alpha = 0$! Do not misalign the coefficients.
  • Signs Alternation: Coefficients always follow $+,-,+,-,+,\dots$ strictly. Double-check when dividing by leading coefficient $a$.

Newton's Magic Trampoline ($S_n = \sum \alpha^n$)

What if an examiner asks you for $\alpha^4 + \beta^4 + \gamma^4$? Multiplying out giant brackets would take ten sheets of paper!

Sir Isaac Newton found a cheat code called a Recurrence Trampoline! Every root satisfies the polynomial equation. When you add all three (or four) equations together, you get:

$a S_n + b S_{n-1} + c S_{n-2} + d S_{n-3} = 0$

Each new power sum simply bounces off the previous ones!

The Root Cloning Machine

Exact Algebraic Steps:
Worked Ex 1.1 Quadratics

One Root is 3× the Other: $x^2 - 2px + p = 0$

Given roots $\alpha$ and $3\alpha$, use $\alpha + 3\alpha = 2p \implies p = 2\alpha$, and $\alpha \times 3\alpha = p \implies p = 3\alpha^2$. Equating gives $4p - 3p^2 = 0 \implies p = \frac{4}{3}$.

Worked Ex 1.13 Fast $S_6$

Evaluating $S_6$ in 10 Seconds: $x^3+5x^2+1=0$

Substitute $y = x^3$. Rearrange to $x^3+1 = -5x^2$ and cube both sides: $(x^3+1)^3 = -125x^6$. This gives $y^3 + 128y^2 + 3y + 1 = 0$. Then $S_6$ of the original is simply $S_2$ of the new cubic: $(-128)^2 - 2(3) = 16378$!

June 2008 Past Paper 9231 P1 Q5

Cubic $x^3+x-1=0 \implies \alpha^6+\beta^6+\gamma^6 = ?$

Rearrange to $x^3-1 = -x$, cube both sides to get $(x^3-1)^3 = -x^3 \implies y^3-3y^2+4y-1=0$. Then $\alpha^6+\beta^6+\gamma^6 = S_2 = 3^2 - 2(4) = 1$!
