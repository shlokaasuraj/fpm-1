ELI6: The Electric Fence & The Sky Ceiling

Imagine you are on a runaway train riding a math track!

  • The Red Electric Fence (Vertical Asymptote): You cannot divide a pizza by zero slices! Whenever the bottom number of the fraction becomes $0$, an invisible electric fence appears. As the train gets closer, it screams and shoots up to the stars ($+\infty$) or plunges into the underworld ($-\infty$), but it can never touch the fence!
  • The Amber Horizon (Horizontal Asymptote): When you walk super far away to the right ($x = +1,000,000$) or left, tiny numbers don't matter anymore. The track settles down on a flat highway at height $y = 1$!
  • The Sneaky Bridge Crossing: Can a curve cross its asymptote? YES! A curve can cross its horizontal asymptote in the middle (like Worked Example 2.2 passing through $(0,0)$), but it can never cross a vertical fence because division by zero is forbidden!

The Diagonal Ski Slope ($y = Ax + B$)

What happens when the top degree ($x^2$) is higher than the bottom ($x$)?

When $x$ grows huge, dividing $x^2$ by $x$ behaves like a tilted line $y = Ax + B$! By doing polynomial long division, you separate the straight runway from the tiny leftover fraction:

$y = \text{Straight Line Runway } (Ax + B) + \frac{\text{Tiny Remainder } C}{dx + e}$

When you fly far away to infinity, the remainder shrinks to zero! The curve hugs the diagonal ski slope!

Exact Algebraic Decomposition:
Discriminant Condition: b² - 4ac < 0
Valid Range of $y$: y ≤ 1 or y ≥ 2
Shaded Red = Forbidden $y$-Band ($\Delta < 0$)

The Forbidden Lava Floor ($b^2 - 4ac < 0$)

Did you know you can find highest and lowest peaks without differentiating?

Imagine asking: "Can this graph ever reach height $y$?" Multiply across to make a quadratic equation in $x$:

$A(y) x^2 + B(y) x + C(y) = 0$

For real points $x$ to exist, the discriminant must NOT be negative! Whenever $\Delta = B^2 - 4AC < 0$, that height $y$ is hot lava—the curve can never touch it!

Worked Ex 2.8 Inequalities

Rational Inequality: $\frac{x+4}{(2x+3)(x+1)} > 1$

Rearranging to $2x^2+4x-1 < 0$ gives roots $x = -1 \pm \frac{\sqrt{6}}{2}$. Crucially, the vertical asymptotes $x = -\frac{3}{2}$ and $x = -1$ cut the interval into two distinct pieces!

Oblique Parameter: $y = \frac{x^2}{x+\lambda}$

Oblique asymptote $y = x - \lambda$. Differentiating yields turning points at $(0,0)$ and $(-2\lambda, -4\lambda)$. Exam asks for separate sketches when $\lambda > 0$ vs $\lambda < 0$.

Maximum Bound: $y = \frac{2x^2-3x-2}{x^2-2x+1} \le \frac{25}{12}$

Repeated pole at $x=1$, horizontal asymptote $y=2$. Cross-multiplying gives $(y-2)x^2 + (3-2y)x + (y+2) = 0$. Discriminant condition $\Delta \ge 0 \implies 12y - 25 \le 0 \implies y \le \frac{25}{12}$!
